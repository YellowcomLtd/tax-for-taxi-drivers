Bookmark this one. The 2026/27 tax year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027, but the deadlines that go with it don't land until much later - which is exactly why they catch drivers out.

## The key dates

- **5 October 2027** - deadline to register for Self Assessment, if you started self-employment at any point during 2026/27 and haven't registered before.
- **31 October 2027** - paper tax return deadline.
- **31 January 2028** - online tax return deadline, and the deadline to pay any balancing tax you owe for 2026/27.
- **31 January 2027** and **31 July 2027** - payments on account, if they apply to you (see below).

## What "payments on account" actually means

If your tax bill is more than £1,000 and less than 80% of your tax was collected at source (which covers almost every self-employed driver), HMRC assumes you'll owe roughly the same again next year, and asks you to pay half of it upfront, twice: once by 31 January and again by 31 July. It's not an extra tax - it's next year's bill, paid early, in two instalments. It's also exactly why your first proper tax year as a driver can involve a genuinely large payment in January: a full year's tax, plus the first payment on account for the year after, both due on the same date.

## What happens if you miss a deadline

Missing the filing deadline is expensive even if you owe no tax at all:

- **£100** automatic penalty for a late return, straight away
- **+£10 a day** after 3 months, up to 90 days (so up to another £900)
- **+5% of the tax due, or £300, whichever is more** - at both the 6-month and 12-month marks

Paying late is a separate penalty on top: **5% of the unpaid tax** at 30 days, 6 months, and 12 months late, plus interest accruing the whole time. None of this is worth risking for the sake of a phone call or an email a few weeks earlier than you'd planned.

## One thing that's about to change for some drivers

If your turnover is £50,000 or more, a new system called Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started applying to you from 6 April 2026, replacing the once-a-year return with quarterly digital updates and its own, different penalty structure.
